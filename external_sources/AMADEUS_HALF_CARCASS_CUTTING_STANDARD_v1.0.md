# Amadeus Farm Half-Carcass Pork Cutting Standard

## 1. Purpose of this document

This document defines the approved pilot cutting structure for **one half pork carcass** and the three Amadeus customer selections built from it.

It is intended to serve as:

- the cutting reference for the butcher;
- the internal source of truth for Amadeus Farm and SAM;
- the basis for catalogue and customer descriptions;
- a clear record of which cuts are alternatives and which cuts are always included; and
- a machine-readable reference for an LLM or software workflow.

The cutting routes are based on the options confirmed by the butcher. The numbers in this document are **Amadeus internal cut codes**. They are not universal industry classification numbers.

> **Core rule:** Every selection represents the cuts produced from one complete half carcass according to one valid combination of cutting routes. It is not a mixed box assembled from unrelated carcasses.

### Pilot boundaries

During the pilot:

- three standard selections will be offered;
- custom cutting is not yet included;
- the belly remains a **whole pork belly**;
- code **5.1.2.2** must always be labelled **Pork Leg Chops / Varkboudtjops**;
- exact cut weights and chop quantities are not guaranteed before the carcass is processed; and
- the butcher-confirmed cutting route takes priority if a practical butchery issue is discovered.

# 3. Half-carcass cutting logic

## 3.1 Canonical decision tree

```text
ONE HALF PORK CARCASS
│
├── 1. NECK / FOREQUARTER — choose exactly ONE route
│   ├── Route 1.1
│   │   └── 1.1 Neck chops
│   │
│   ├── Route 1.2 — both cuts are received together
│   │   ├── 1.2.1 Thick rib chops
│   │   └── 1.2.2 Shoulder chops
│   │
│   └── Route 1.3 — both cuts are received together
│       ├── 1.3.1 Boneless neck steaks
│       └── 1.3.2 Additional forequarter stew meat
│
├── 2. LOIN — always included
│   └── 2 Loin chops
│
├── 3. RIB SECTION — choose exactly ONE
│   ├── 3.1 Whole rib
│   └── 3.2 Bone-in rib rashers
│
├── 4. BELLY — always included during the pilot
│   └── 4 Whole pork belly
│
├── 5. LEG — choose exactly ONE route
│   ├── 5 Whole leg
│   ├── 5.1.1 Two half legs
│   └── Split-leg route — both cuts are received together
│       ├── 5.1.2.1 One half leg
│       └── 5.1.2.2 Leg chops
│
├── 6. SHANKS — choose exactly ONE
│   ├── 6.1 Whole shanks (Eisbein)
│   └── 6.2 Cut shanks
│
└── 7. STANDARD STEW MEAT — always included
    └── 7 Stew meat
```

## 3.2 The simplest way to understand the carcass

Every valid half-carcass selection must contain:

1. one neck/forequarter route;
2. loin chops;
3. one rib preparation;
4. one whole pork belly;
5. one leg preparation;
6. one shank preparation; and
7. the standard stew-meat portion.

# 5. Non-negotiable compatibility rules

## 5.1 Neck and forequarter

Choose exactly one of the following:

- **1.1** Neck chops; or
- **1.2.1 + 1.2.2** Thick rib chops and shoulder chops; or
- **1.3.1 + 1.3.2** Boneless neck steaks and additional forequarter stew meat.

The following combinations are invalid:

- neck chops plus thick rib chops;
- neck chops plus shoulder chops;
- neck chops plus boneless neck steaks;
- neck steaks plus thick rib or shoulder chops; or
- any mixture of Routes 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3.

## 5.2 Rib section

Choose exactly one:

- **3.1** Whole rib; or
- **3.2** Bone-in rib rashers.

A customer cannot receive a whole rib and rib rashers from the same half carcass.

## 5.3 Belly

- **4 Whole pork belly** is included in every pilot selection.
- Rib rashers under code 3.2 remain available because they come from the rib route specified by the butcher.

## 5.4 Leg

Choose exactly one complete route:

- **5** Whole leg; or
- **5.1.1** Two half legs; or
- **5.1.2.1 + 5.1.2.2** One half leg and leg chops.

Do not combine a whole leg with half legs or leg chops.

## 5.5 Shanks

Choose exactly one:

- **6.1** Whole shanks (Eisbein); or
- **6.2** Cut shanks.

## 5.6 Cuts included in every selection

The following codes are mandatory in all three selections:

- **2** Loin chops;
- **4** Whole pork belly; and
- **7** Standard stew meat.

# 6. Approved pilot selections

## 6.1 Set A — Amadeus Signature Collection

### Positioning

The most versatile household selection. It balances quick-cooking cuts, smaller roasting portions and slow-cooking meat.

### Why this selection works

- Boneless neck steaks and loin chops provide convenient quick-cooking meals.
- Rib rashers provide a practical braai or oven option without using the whole-rib route.
- Two half legs are easier for an ordinary household to cook and store than one very large leg.
- Cut shanks and the two stew allocations provide useful slow-cooking options.
- The whole belly gives the customer a premium roasting cut.

### Suggested customer description

> **A versatile half-carcass selection for the family table.** It combines boneless neck steaks, loin chops and rib rashers with two manageable half-leg roasts, whole pork belly, cut shanks and generous stew-meat portions.

### Best suited to

- households wanting the widest everyday variety;
- customers who prefer smaller roasting portions;
- customers wanting both quick-cooking and slow-cooking options; and
- first-time half-carcass buyers.

# 8. Why the pilot should use three selections

Three selections are enough because they deliberately use all three available forequarter routes:

- Set A tests the boneless neck-steak route;
- Set B tests the thick-rib and shoulder-chop route; and
- Set C tests the neck-chop route.

A fourth standard selection would probably duplicate one of these routes and make the pilot harder to explain, cut, label and evaluate. Custom cutting can be introduced later once the team has real yield, sales and customer-preference data.

# 9. Correct English and Afrikaans terminology

The table below contains the preferred terms for catalogues, butcher instructions and system data.

| English term | Preferred Afrikaans term | Usage note |
|---|---|---|
| Half pork carcass | **Halwe varkkarkas** | “Carcase” is an accepted alternate English spelling, but use one spelling consistently in system data. |
| Neck / forequarter | **Nek / voorstuk** | “Voorstuk” is used here for the connected forequarter cutting area. |
| Pork neck chops | **Varknektjops** | Write as one compound word. |
| Thick rib chops | **Dikribtjops** | Write as one compound word. |
| Pork shoulder chops | **Varkskouertjops** | Do not call these a whole shoulder. |
| Boneless pork neck steaks | **Ontbeende varkneksteaks** | Preferred over “deboned neck chops.” Once boneless, they are sold as neck steaks. |
| Additional forequarter stew meat | **Ekstra varkstowevleis uit die voorstuk** | This is additional to code 7. |
| Pork loin chops | **Varklendetjops** | Established Afrikaans culinary term. |
| Whole pork rib | **Heel varkrib** | “Heel varkribrak” may be used when the rack presentation is important. |
| Bone-in pork rib rashers | **Varkribrashers met been** | Keep “rashers” because it is the familiar South African retail term for this butcher-confirmed rib preparation. |
| Whole pork belly | **Heel varklies** | **Varklies** is the preferred Afrikaans culinary term. **Varkbuik** is understandable as a secondary synonym. |
| Whole pork leg | **Heel varkboud** | Large roasting cut. |
| Half pork leg | **Halwe varkboud** | Two pieces: **twee halwe varkboude**. |
| Pork leg chops | **Varkboudtjops** | This is the approved name for code 5.1.2.2. |
| Whole pork shanks (Eisbein) | **Heel varkskenkels (Eisbein)** | “Eisbein” is included as the familiar customer term. Do not claim that the product is cured or smoked unless the butcher has actually processed it that way. |
| Cut pork shanks | **Gesnyde varkskenkels** | Suitable for slow cooking. |
| Pork stew meat | **Varkstowevleis** | Use for the product category. |
| Pork stew cubes | **Varkstoweblokkies** | Use only when the butcher cuts the meat into uniform cubes. |

## Terms not to use in the pilot

Do not use:

- deboned neck chops;
- whole shoulder or half shoulder;
- whole rib and rib rashers in the same half-carcass selection;
- “bulk pack” as the customer-facing name; or
- any claim that the whole shanks are cured or smoked unless the butcher has actually processed them that way.

# 10. Butcher and packaging instructions for the pilot

Before processing the pilot carcasses, agree and record:

- the standard chop thickness;
- the standard thickness and presentation of rib rashers;
- whether each cut remains skin-on or skin-off;
- whether the leg remains bone-in;
- whether the whole rib is trimmed and squared as a rack;
- the approximate number of pieces per retail package;
- whether packaging is vacuum sealed or otherwise wrapped;
- the English and Afrikaans label used on every package;
- the weight of every individual package;
- the total packed weight returned from each half carcass;
- the cutting and packing dates; and
- any bones, fat, skin, feet, offal or trim that fall outside the confirmed customer cut list.

## Required pilot records

For each half carcass, record:

| Field | Example / instruction |
|---|---|
| Carcass or animal reference | Unique internal ID |
| Customer selection | Set A, Set B or Set C |
| Live weight | Record if available |
| Half-carcass weight received by butcher | Record actual weight |
| Cut code | Use the codes in this document |
| Cut name | English and Afrikaans |
| Package count | Actual number of packages |
| Piece count | Actual number where useful |
| Package weight | Weight of every package |
| Total packed weight | Sum of all returned cuts |
| Processing loss / unreturned material | Record and explain |
| Customer feedback | Quality, usefulness, preferred cuts and complaints |

> Do not advertise an exact number of chops until the pilot has produced reliable results. Carcass dimensions, chop thickness, trimming and butchery technique will change the final quantity.

# 11. Rules for SAM, CHARLIE or another LLM

An LLM using this document must follow these rules:

1. Treat each selection as a complete half-carcass cutting route.
2. Never combine alternatives that the carcass cannot produce together.
3. Always include codes **2, 4 and 7** in every standard selection.
4. Always describe code **4** as **Whole Pork Belly / Heel varklies**.
5. Always label code **5.1.2.2** as **Pork Leg Chops / Varkboudtjops**.
6. Do not invent a whole shoulder or half shoulder option.
7. Remember that code **1.3.2** is additional stew meat and code **7** is the standard stew allocation.
8. Use internal cut codes only in butcher, operations and system views.
9. Remove cut codes from all customer-facing lists.
10. Do not guarantee exact cut weights, package counts or chop counts before processing.
11. Describe Set B as **braai-led**, not as containing only braai meat.
12. Use **Whole Pork Shanks (Eisbein)** as the customer-facing cut name, but do not claim that they are cured or smoked unless they have actually been processed that way.
13. Tell customers that custom cuts are planned for a later phase but are not part of the current pilot.
14. Use the approved working names unless the owner formally changes the naming route.

## Eisbein accuracy note

In common use, customers may refer to a whole pork shank or hock as “Eisbein.” Technically, Eisbein normally refers to a cured hock and it may also be smoked. In this document, the word is placed in brackets as the familiar customer name for the whole shank cut:

- **Whole Pork Shanks (Eisbein)**
- **Heel varkskenkels (Eisbein)**
