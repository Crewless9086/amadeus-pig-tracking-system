# Pilot CV-001 — Petra

## Working title

**How Petra Tamed a Flash Flood**

## Approved channel promise

Chronicle Vault reveals how ordinary systems kept societies alive and what happened when they failed.

## Research question

How did water engineering make dense settlement possible at Petra while reducing the danger of sudden floods, and what does the surviving evidence actually allow us to claim?

## Viewer question

How can a city in an arid mountain basin survive both too little water and too much water arriving at once?

## Original thesis

Petra’s achievement was not a single dam or aqueduct. Its inhabitants managed a watershed as a layered system: landscape features slowed and redirected runoff; channels and pipelines conveyed water; settling features improved supply; and cisterns/reservoirs stored it. The same system turned water from an intermittent threat into an urban resource, but it depended on distributed maintenance and changed over centuries.

## Hypothesis tested

A place-led engineering story with immediate survival stakes can attract history and engineering viewers without mystery sensationalism.

## Scope

- Petra and its immediate watershed, chiefly the Nabataean and early Roman periods.
- Flood moderation, supply, storage and distribution.
- The Siq/diversion system and Wadi Hremiyyeh as clear examples.
- Water as survival infrastructure and, briefly, as urban display.

## Exclusions

- No claim that Nabataeans invented all component technologies.
- No precise population, network-length, dam/cistern count or daily water-volume claim unless a final source supports the exact figure.
- No single-cause story about Petra’s decline.
- No implication that ancient practice can be copied directly into modern flood management without current engineering.
- No fabricated reenactment presented as real footage.

## Runtime and format

Target 13–15 minutes, narrated documentary with original maps, cross-sections and flow diagrams. Private package only.
