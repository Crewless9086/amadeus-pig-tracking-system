# Petra Synthetic-Narration Evaluation Plan

**State:** Evaluation design only. No provider, account, payment, voice generation, recording or production use is authorised.

## Objective

Determine whether a synthetic voice can support repeatable StoryWorks production without making Charl the default narrator, while preserving editorial quality, pronunciation control, commercial rights and explicit owner control.

## Evidence matrix

| Criterion | Evidence to collect after separate approval | Pass condition | Current state |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voice quality and suitability | Same owner-approved, non-sensitive test passage across candidates; blind human ratings for clarity, authority, warmth, fatigue and artefacts | Meets an owner-set score and has no material trust or comprehension defect | Unknown—not evaluated |
| Pronunciation control | Documented lexicon/phoneme controls tested against the approved pronunciation sheet | Every named place and technical term is repeatable across rerenders | Unknown—not evaluated |
| Commercial-use rights | Exact plan, licence, output ownership, YouTube/commercial-use and derivative-edit terms retained | Rights explicitly cover intended monetised video use and retained masters | Unknown—not researched |
| Provider terms | Current terms, privacy/data retention, model-training use, termination/export and unilateral-change controls | Owner accepts terms and data treatment; evidence snapshot retained | Unknown—not researched |
| Disclosure | Current YouTube rules and final audio context reviewed | Required disclosure is known and can be applied without misleading viewers | Unknown pending route and final assets |
| Cost per finished video | Subscription/usage, retries, direction, cleanup, mastering and human QA recorded against one finished-video runtime | Evidence-backed ZAR cost range fits the approved unit-economics envelope | Unknown—no pricing authorised |
| Repeatability and automation | Same script/version/lexicon can be reproduced; exceptions and manual QA are logged | Stable results with a defined human approval gate and recoverable source files | Unknown—not tested |
| Owner approval controls | Proposed flow separates script approval, test generation, voice approval and final-use approval | No generation or use without the exact recorded owner gate | Plan defined; implementation not authorised |

## Controlled comparison design

1. Freeze an owner-approved script excerpt only after editorial and pronunciation sign-off.
2. Separately request authority to research named providers and current terms; do not create accounts.
3. Build a comparable evidence sheet with source dates, exact plan assumptions and ZAR conversions clearly labelled.
4. If separately authorised, generate only the approved short test passage—never the full episode.
5. Blind-review the test against the same approved human-quality rubric.
6. Recommend owner narration, an approved human narrator or synthetic narration from evidence; do not infer scalability from one pleasant sample.

## Decision rule

Recommend the route with the strongest repeatable quality and rights certainty at an evidenced total cost per finished video. Synthetic narration is not preferred merely because it is automatable; human narration is not preferred merely because it sounds natural. Any route must pass pronunciation, commercial-rights, disclosure, cost and owner-control gates.

## Remaining owner gates

- Authorise or reject named-provider desk research.
- Approve the comparison passage and scoring rubric.
- Approve any provider terms, account, payment and bounded test generation separately.
- Approve or reject the selected voice for private episode use.
- Approve final narration only after full-script and pronunciation sign-off.
