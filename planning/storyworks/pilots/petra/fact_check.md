# Petra Fact-Check Report

## Verdict

**PASS WITH REQUIRED WORDING CONTROLS**

The central thesis is supported by official heritage descriptions and peer-reviewed hydraulic/archaeological studies. The script must preserve the distinction between observed structures, modern engineering analysis and programme synthesis.

## Required controls

- Use “Nabataean Petra” when attribution is secure; use “Petra’s inhabitants and administrators” where works may span later periods.
- Say “helped reduce/moderate flood risk,” never “stopped all floods.”
- Treat the whole-system framing as a synthesis, not a recovered ancient plan.
- Do not state an exact peak population, network length, number of cisterns/dams, flow rate, storm speed or storage capacity.
- Do not call Petra lifeless desert; the landscape is arid/semi-arid with seasonal rainfall, springs and managed catchments.
- Do not claim water engineering alone created, sustained or ended Petra.
- Explain that technology moved across connected societies; avoid “ahead of its time.”
- Label the inferred sequence of what a raindrop encounters as an explanatory reconstruction.

## Claims removed or held

- “A city of 30,000”: estimates exist, but unnecessary to the thesis.
- “200 kilometres of pipe / 200 cisterns / 40 dams”: widely repeated secondary figures were not needed and were not adopted.
- “The Muthlim tunnel saved the Treasury”: too absolute; use a bounded diversion/flood-risk claim.
- “The 363 earthquake destroyed the water system and ended Petra”: reductive and outside this pilot’s supported scope.
- “Every drop was captured”: rhetorical overstatement.

## Speculation and uncertainty labels

- “Researchers reconstruct…”
- “The surviving evidence suggests…”
- “In the Wadi Hremiyyeh catchment, modern modelling indicates…”
- “The chronology of individual components is not always certain.”
- “This episode’s argument is…”
