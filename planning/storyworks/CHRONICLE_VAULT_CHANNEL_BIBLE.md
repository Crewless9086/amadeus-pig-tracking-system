# Chronicle Vault Channel Positioning

## Proposed audience

- **Primary viewer:** English-speaking, intellectually curious adults aged roughly 25–54 who enjoy history, engineering, archaeology and practical explanation.
- **Motivation:** Understand how societies actually functioned, not only who ruled or fought.
- **Viewing context:** Intentional evening/weekend viewing, often on TV or desktop; willing to watch 12–20 minutes for a satisfying explanation.
- **Geography/language:** English-first global channel, initially packaging for audiences in South Africa, the UK, US, Canada, Australia and other English-proficient markets. Do not claim local-market distribution before analytics exists.
- **Competing attention:** Practical Engineering, Fall of Civilizations, History Time, Invicta, Told in Stone, Stefan Milo, Timeline, History Hit and premium streaming documentaries.
- **Why choose Chronicle Vault:** A clear practical question, a system diagram viewers can understand, a human consequence and an evidence-led answer.
- **Why StoryWorks can serve it:** The programme can combine structured research, business/process thinking, agricultural familiarity and disciplined provenance. Historical authority must still be earned through sources and specialist review.

### Option A — Systems That Kept Civilisations Alive

Promise:

> We reveal how ordinary systems—water, food, roads, soil, sanitation and trade—kept societies alive, and what happened when they failed.

Strengths: immediately practical, repeatable across regions and eras, visually diagrammable, differentiated from leader/battle history, and closely aligned with agricultural/process thinking.

Risk: can sound dry unless every episode is built around people, stakes and a decisive constraint.

### Option B — Forgotten Technologies That Worked

Promise:

> We reconstruct overlooked technologies and show what they could—and could not—really do.

Strengths: strong object-led thumbnails, high curiosity and natural myth correction.

Risk: crowded “ancient tech/mystery” packaging, temptation to exaggerate, and potential dependence on hard-to-license site/artefact visuals.

### Option C — Decisions That Changed Civilisations

Promise:

> We examine the practical choices that redirected societies, then trace the consequences.

Strengths: human agency, narrative conflict and business relevance.

Risk: more abstract, greater causation/speculation burden and easier drift into politics, warfare or counterfactual entertainment.

## Recommendation

Recommend Option A, subject to owner approval. It has the clearest repeatable audience promise and strongest fit with StoryWorks’ credible capabilities. Options B and C become episode lenses inside the promise, not separate pillars.

## Editorial rules

- One practical viewer question per episode.
- One explicit thesis, not a list of facts.
- Explain mechanism, constraint, human consequence and failure/legacy.
- Use multiple credible sources; distinguish evidence, interpretation and uncertainty.
- Avoid “they hid this,” “impossible,” “secret truth,” and fabricated certainty.
- No unrelated space, generic sustainability commentary or alternate history.
- Counterfactuals only as brief, clearly labelled analysis when evidence supports the boundary.
- Target 12–16 minutes for the pilot; quality over cadence.

## Packaging direction

Title pattern: **How [system] kept [place/people] alive—and why it failed**.

Thumbnail: one recognisable site/object plus a restrained schematic overlay showing flow or failure; two-to-four words; no misleading reconstruction presented as evidence.
