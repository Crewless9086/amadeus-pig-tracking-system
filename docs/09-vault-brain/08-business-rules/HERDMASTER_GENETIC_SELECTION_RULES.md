# HERDMASTER Genetic Selection Rules

Status: owner-approved operating policy, 11 August 2026.

## Business purpose

HERDMASTER must improve the herd generation by generation. A mating recommendation is not a workload-balancing calculation. It must identify the best-supported complementary sow-boar pairing from attributable reproductive, litter, survival, growth, structural, welfare and lineage evidence, then convert those choices into a physically workable breeding round.

The owner-visible result is a concise Afrikaans worklist that Charl can use with his father. It must explain why each primary pairing advances the herd, name a reserve, distinguish proven evidence from a controlled trial, and sequence any females that cannot enter the immediate physical group.

## Foundation-herd declaration

Charl declares the current foundation breeding population operationally unrelated unless an attributable record proves a relationship. This declaration applies to the current foundation females and the current boars Bola, Tyson and Prince because their earlier biological dam/sire histories are unavailable.

This is an owner-declared operating baseline, not a scientific claim of verified ancestry. HERDMASTER must:

- preserve and disclose the foundation limitation;
- continue to exclude every known direct, shared-ancestor or conflicting relationship;
- never manufacture missing parents or ancestors;
- never apply this declaration automatically to later animals;
- record exact sow and boar parentage for every attributable litter and offspring from this point forward.

Once descendants enter the breeding population, their known lineage is binding. Parent-offspring, full-sibling and half-sibling pairings are prohibited. HERDMASTER must also warn against excessive concentration of one foundation boar's descendants.

## Selection hierarchy

HERDMASTER evaluates an eligible female in this order:

1. Welfare, health, lifecycle and reproductive eligibility.
2. Known relationship and identity exclusions.
3. Exact previous sow-boar combination results.
4. Live-born and successfully weaned survival, not litter size alone.
5. Offspring growth compared at supportably similar ages.
6. Sow recovery, body condition and attributable mothering evidence.
7. Recurring weak-litter, mortality, structural or welfare signals.
8. Avoidance of repeating a demonstrably poor combination.
9. Useful genetic diversity and avoidance of excessive line concentration.
10. Evidence strength and controlled learning from an unproven boar.
11. Physical group capacity and timing, applied only after genetic and production suitability.

No single service count, workload count or absence of prior failure may establish genetic superiority.

## Evidence interpretation

For each possible pair, preserve these separately:

- `known`: attributable services, litters, born alive, weaned/surviving outcomes, weights, growth, losses, observations and lineage;
- `calculated`: survival proportions, comparable-age growth summaries, repeat-pair concentration and workload;
- `owner-declared`: foundation-unrelated baseline and current operating capacity;
- `unknown`: unavailable ancestry, incomplete physical observations or unsupported performance;
- `prohibited inference`: fertility, genetic merit, soundness, heat, pregnancy or parentage from silence.

Litter outcome reflects the sow, boar and management environment. HERDMASTER must not attribute a weak or strong litter solely to the boar when evidence cannot separate those contributions. Repeated results across different females strengthen boar evidence; repeated results for one sow strengthen pair-specific evidence.

## Evidence classes

Every recommended pairing receives one plain-language class:

- **Proven repeat:** this exact sow-boar combination has attributable strong outcomes worth repeating.
- **Supported cross:** different attributable histories support the pairing, but the exact combination is less proven.
- **Corrective cross:** a prior weak combination is deliberately not repeated and the alternative addresses the supported concern.
- **Controlled trial:** the boar or exact combination is insufficiently proven and is assigned deliberately to learn without dominating the breeding round.
- **Limited evidence:** a useful provisional choice is possible, but decisive production evidence remains incomplete.

## Prince and future new boars

Prince's low service count means he is less proven, not better. His lower workload may prevent overuse of Bola or Tyson, but it must not make him the automatic primary boar.

Initially, Prince should receive a small, purposeful trial group chosen from females whose reliable maternal and litter history makes the resulting evidence interpretable. His allocation expands only when attributable offspring survival, growth, soundness and consistency support it. One litter is evidence, but not final proof.

The same controlled-trial rule applies to every future new boar.

## Whole-round allocation

HERDMASTER first determines the best and reserve pairing for each female from genetic and production evidence. It then optimises the complete breeding round so that:

- a single independently ranked boar cannot silently become primary for every female;
- known successful combinations are retained where justified;
- known weak combinations are not repeated without an explicit reason;
- controlled trials remain bounded;
- useful foundation-line diversity is preserved;
- physical capacity is respected without overriding pairing quality.

The initial owner operating limit is normally two or three females with one boar in an immediate group. This is a configurable farm-capacity rule, not a biological truth and not a requirement to divide females equally. Overflow females must remain on a sequenced next-group worklist until mated, held or no longer eligible.

## Backlog prevention

After governed weaning and any supported recovery period, every breeding female must enter one visible state:

- active cycle or pregnancy monitoring;
- nursing or recovery;
- ready for observation;
- ready for mating allocation;
- allocated to an immediate group;
- sequenced in the next group;
- owner, welfare, medical or withdrawal hold;
- replacement/retirement review.

An eligible-but-unmated female must remain visible in Oom Sakkie's daily/weekly management work until an attributable mating is recorded or a genuine hold is established. Pen location cannot clear or create that obligation.

## Observation and recording journey

Reuse the existing append-only `pig_observation_events` breeding-observation rail. The ordinary owner journey must support natural Oom Sakkie reporting and the Breeding Attention/mating-board recovery surface for:

- standing heat or no heat observed;
- body-condition score;
- legs, feet and normal movement;
- visible injury, build or concern;
- temperament and suitability concern;
- factual notes and follow-up.

One grouped farm observation may name several females, but each fact must bind to the exact canonical female and observation time. Observation does not itself create a mating, prove fertility or pregnancy, or clear a medical/welfare hold.

An actual service requires the exact sow, exact boar and attributable service date through the governed mating-recording path. The resulting litter and offspring must retain both parent identities so HERDMASTER can enforce real lineage rules in future generations.

## Required presentation

The Afrikaans owner worklist must be grouped for physical use, for example:

- `Nou by Bola`
- `Nou by Tyson`
- `Prince - beheerde proefgroep`
- `Volgende groep`
- `Waarnemings benodig`

Show animal names first. For each female show the primary reason, evidence class, reserve boar and material limitation. Generated IDs are supporting audit detail, not the main farm language.

## Completion

Source, CI, deployment, replay or a Telegram send is not completion. Completion requires a fresh canonical assessment, a practical capacity-aware owner worklist delivered through Oom Sakkie, and application-visible observation/backlog follow-through. No recommendation may be reported as a recorded mating.
