# Petra Source Packet

**Research date:** 2026-07-23

## Source quality key

- **A:** official heritage authority or peer-reviewed research directly about Petra.
- **B:** established scholarly/engineering institution summarising research.
- **C:** contextual lead only; do not use alone for a material claim.

## Core sources

### S01 — UNESCO World Heritage Centre: Petra

- URL: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/326/
- Quality: A
- Supports: Petra’s location and historical role; arid settlement; surviving channels, tunnels, diversion dams, cisterns and reservoirs; seasonal rain control/conservation; present vulnerability to flash floods; need for continued monitoring and maintenance.
- Limitation: heritage synthesis, not a component-by-component chronology.

### S02 — Ortloff, “The Water Supply and Distribution System of the Nabataean City of Petra (Jordan), 300 BC–AD 300”

- URL: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0959774305000053
- Journal: *Cambridge Archaeological Journal* 15(1), 2005, pp. 93–109.
- Quality: A
- Supports: mapped supply/distribution system; balance of reservoir storage and continuous-flow pipelines; year-round supply objective; settling basins; flow-control design; technologies adapted through regional contacts plus local innovation.
- Limitation: some reconstructions and design inferences are the author’s engineering analysis, not direct Nabataean written testimony.

### S03 — Akasheh, “Ancient and Modern Watershed Management in Petra”

- URL: https://doi.org/10.2307/3210850
- Journal: *Near Eastern Archaeology* 65(4), 2002.
- Quality: A
- Supports: seasonal flash flooding threatened ancient and modern Petra; surveys of dams and watershed modelling; ancient works demonstrate flood-control intent.
- Limitation: abstract-level access during this package; do not quote details unavailable in the accessible text.

### S04 — Al-Rawabdeh et al., “Hydrological assessment and management implications for the ancient Nabataean flood control system in Petra, Jordan”

- URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2021.126583
- Journal: *Journal of Hydrology*, 2021.
- Quality: A
- Supports: Wadi Hremiyyeh contributes substantially to flooding at Treasury Plaza; high-resolution terrain, rainfall and water-level work; terraces/check structures reduced sudden runoff; limits of historical hydrological data.
- Limitation: conclusions are catchment-specific and should not be universalised to all Petra.

### S05 — Hamarneh et al., “The Nabatean Flood Control System of Wadi Hremiyyeh, Petra: Restoring and Learning from the Past”

- URL: https://doi.org/10.1086/721013
- Journal: *Near Eastern Archaeology* 85(3), 2022.
- Quality: A
- Supports: restoration of a system that had protected Treasury Plaza; headwater flow moderation prevented sudden surges; multidisciplinary modern conservation/restoration.
- Limitation: modern restoration is not proof that every ancient component functioned identically throughout Petra’s history.

### S06 — Ortloff, “Water engineering at Petra: recreating the decision process underlying the Wadi Mataha pipeline system”

- URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jas.2014.01.015
- Journal: *Journal of Archaeological Science* 44, 2014, pp. 91–97.
- Quality: A
- Supports: distant springs, mountainous wadis and seasonal rainfall created distinct supply constraints; Wadi Mataha design choices show hydraulic reasoning; documentary evidence is limited.
- Limitation: inferred decision process; phrase as analytical reconstruction.

### S07 — Jungmann, “‘Ain Braq aqueduct: new insights into Petra’s urban water management”

- URL: https://doi.org/10.1080/00758914.2025.2592501
- Journal: *Levant* 58(1), published online 2025.
- Quality: A
- Supports: continuing discoveries can revise the picture; supply supported more than bare survival and interacted with urban planning.
- Limitation: recent article access is limited; use only accessible abstract claims.

### S08 — ACOR: Petra Garden and Pool Complex

- URL: https://publications.acorjordan.org/2014/10/29/pipe-dreams-and-paradise-the-water-system-of-the-petra-garde-and-pool-complex-by-dr-leigh-ann-bedal/
- Quality: B
- Supports: excavated pool, island pavilion and cultivated terrace; water surplus also communicated abundance, leisure and power.
- Limitation: public lecture summary; use as a supporting example, not the hydraulic backbone.

### S09 — Jordan Department of Antiquities: Petra

- URL: https://doa.gov.jo/EN/ListDetails/World_Heritage_Sites_in_Jordan/1158/2
- Quality: A
- Supports: official Jordanian heritage framing; channels, tunnels, diversion dams, basins and cisterns controlled/stored seasonal rainfall.
- Limitation: concise institutional summary.

## Context and uncertainty

- Petra’s water landscape accumulated and changed across Nabataean, Roman, Byzantine and later periods; surviving components should not be narrated as one moment of construction.
- Exact capacity, population served and total network extent vary among reconstructions.
- “The Nabataeans invented” is usually too strong; their achievement involved selection, adaptation, integration and local innovation.
- Flood-control performance depended on location, event size and maintenance. “Prevented all floods” is false.
- Decline was multi-causal and temporally complex; this pilot does not assign collapse to a single earthquake or failed aqueduct.
