# Amadeus StoryWorks Phase 0 Executive Decision Pack

**Decision date:** 2026-07-23
**Public actions taken:** None
**Revenue generated:** R0

## Executive verdict

**Proceed conditionally to a bounded, private three-pilot production test; do
not treat the business as validated and do not launch a channel yet.**

StoryWorks is intended as a standalone, self-sustaining YouTube media
enterprise. The Chronicle Vault is its first validation property, not
necessarily its permanent limit. Current work proves neither audience demand
nor monetisation, revenue, cash receipt or distributable profit. The durable
evidence sequence is defined in
[`BUSINESS_STATE_LADDER.md`](BUSINESS_STATE_LADDER.md).

The category has proven audience demand, but broad history is crowded and quality expectations are high. Chronicle Vault has a credible testable gap in story-led explanations of the practical systems that kept societies alive. Commercial viability remains unproven until original pilots can be made at acceptable cost and public evidence shows qualified viewing demand.

## Recommended positioning

> **We reveal how ordinary systems—water, food, roads, soil, sanitation and trade—kept societies alive, and what happened when they failed.**

This is clearer and more defensible than generic forgotten technology or broad civilisation-changing decisions. Those become episode lenses, not separate channel pillars.

Primary viewer: English-speaking, intellectually curious adults who enjoy history, archaeology and engineering but want to understand mechanisms and consequences, not only rulers and battles.

## Evidence summary

- Current comparison channels demonstrate large audiences for engineering explanation and serious history: Practical Engineering (~4.8M), Timeline (~5.8M), Kings and Generals (~4.2M), Epic History TV (~3.1M), History Hit (~1.9M), Fall of Civilizations (~1.5M) and History Time (~1.3M), based on current public/third-party observations.
- The 20-channel comparison and accessible recent-video listings found durable “how/why it worked or failed” packaging, 12–25 minute accessible explainers and strong use of maps/diagrams.
- Saturated lanes include battles, leaders, Rome/Egypt generalism, generic lost technology and mystery sensationalism.
- The practical civilian system, maintenance burden and decision trade-off are less consistently owned.
- YouTube policy permits authored documentary work but puts mass-produced/repetitive “inauthentic content,” insufficiently transformed reused content, unsupported rights and undisclosed realistic synthetic media at risk.

Unknown until an authorised market test: impressions, CTR, retention, subscriber conversion, returning viewers, RPM and sustainable cost per watch hour. A uniform 100-row recent-video dataset also remains incomplete because a normal browser session was unavailable and comparable public fields could not be collected without bulk scraping; that gap is disclosed rather than filled with estimates.

## Proposed pilots

1. **Petra:** water engineering, visual place identity and immediate survival stakes.
2. **Rome’s grain system:** food/logistics complexity inside a familiar civilisation.
3. **Inca roads without wheels:** non-Mediterranean network story challenging a common assumption.

The set tests different hypotheses without diluting the channel promise. Rice terraces is the reserve.

## Main risks

- Quality may require more human editing or specialist review than the lean ceiling supports.
- Public-domain/licensed historical visuals may be less usable than expected.
- New-channel distribution can be weak even with good content.
- Familiar history topics can produce clicks but blur differentiation.
- AI-assisted visuals or narration could create trust, disclosure or inauthentic-content risk if overused.
- Ad-only payback is slow; YPP qualification precedes revenue sharing.
- Third-party public channel observations do not reveal private economics or retention.
- The recent-video sample is directional, not the requested uniform 100-row dataset; complete it through a normal browser or approved official-data route before publication.

## Validation gates

**Go:** three distinct packages pass factual, narrative, rights, disclosure and technical QA within the ceiling; the manual workflow is repeatable.

**Revise once:** 71–100 hours, correctable rights/clarity problems, or one identifiable production bottleneck.

**Stop/return for decision:** >100 hours per pilot, >R7,500 cash or >R35,000 economic programme cost, >12 weeks, unsupported thesis, unresolved rights, generic synthetic output, or required material repositioning.

After authorised publication, directional go signals include meaningful platform impressions, CTR ≥4.5%, first-30-second retention ≥65%, average percentage viewed ≥40%, and subscriber conversion ≥1% on at least one pilot. Interpret sample sizes and traffic sources; one video is not definitive.

No second channel or software build without three-pilot evidence, two credible demand signals, a quantified repeated bottleneck, a costed architecture case and separate owner approval.

## Decisions required

1. Approve, revise or reject the recommended channel promise.
2. Approve the three proposed pilots or substitute the rice-terraces reserve for one.
3. Approve, reduce or reject the proposed R7,500 cash / R35,000 economic / 12-week ceiling.
4. If approved, authorise private Phase 1 package production only. This does not authorise channel creation, spend beyond the ceiling, contractors, publishing, AdSense, outreach, CORE integration, PR creation, merge or deployment.
